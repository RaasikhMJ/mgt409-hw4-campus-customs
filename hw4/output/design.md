# Design — Campus Customs

Two parts of the shop are meant to feel like a counter, not a form. A signed-in shopper can see their money while they browse. Champagne glasses mark both how much swag a piece has and how loyal a purchase history has become.

## Spending, kept in view

**What was added.** The bag mark sits on the homepage and in the navigation, so the queue is one click away on every page. Opening it shows each queued piece in the same large-photo layout as the product page, with the size, the count, and the line total. A signed-in shopper also gets a spending bar under the navigation. A slider sets the limit from $25 to $2,500, in $25 steps. The bar adds what is in the bag to what they have counted as purchased, then says what is left or how far over the limit they are. The bag page repeats that tally. The limit and the purchased lines stay with that account in the browser. Guests can still queue pieces. The limit appears after they sign in.

**Why it helps.** A shopper can decide while they are still looking, instead of discovering the total after they have fallen for three pieces. The bag and the purchases sit on one number, so the limit is part of browsing. The house gets fewer surprised carts and a clearer moment to stop, or to go on, before checkout.

## Champagne for swag and for loyalty

**What was added.** The old swag bars are gone. Each piece now shows five champagne flutes. The rating fills that many with brass champagne and a few rising bubbles. The rest stay empty glass, and the number is still printed as 1–5. On the spending bar and on the bag page, a signed-in shopper also gets a loyalty toast. Each piece they count as purchased fills one more glass, up to five. Zero purchases leaves five empty glasses and the line “Purchases pour the next glass.”

**Why it helps.** The flutes are a quick celebration of the piece: a 5 is a full toast, a 1 is a single glass, and both are readable before the description. The loyalty glasses do the same job for the relationship. Buying is marked like a toast at the counter, not only as a dollar line, which gives a reason to come back and fill the next glass. The imagery stays in the shop’s brass and bone, so the celebration belongs to Campus Customs.

## The stylist’s mark

**What was added.** The chat control is no longer a plain brass label. It carries Goldie, the stylist: a round badge with her profile in a feathered hat and tailored jacket, ringed in brass. Beside the badge, “Swag Stylist” sits in small brass capitals with her name, Goldie, underneath in the serif. The top line changes to “Close Stylist” when the counter is open.

**Why it helps.** The corner reads as a person who can measure a fit, which is the job of the chat. A shopper looking for a size or a cut can find that counter without hunting for a generic button, and a stylist with a name feels like someone to ask, not a form to fill.
